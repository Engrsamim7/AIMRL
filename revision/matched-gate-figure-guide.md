# How to read the matched-training figures

These are eight figures from the new matched-adaptation experiment. They are separate from the earlier 20-page frozen-model collection. Numerical outcomes and statistical decisions belong in `matched-gate-results/README.md`; this guide explains what each visualization can establish.

ALL has neither learned admission nor safeguards; SAFE has only safeguards; VALUE has only learned admission; COMBINED has both. SAFE and COMBINED use identical safeguards in this study. The earlier SAFE-ALL baseline is a different implementation and its percentages must not be inserted into these comparisons.

1. **Primary effect and uncertainty.** COMBINED minus SAFE full-delivery rate, equally averaged across eight cases. The unit is percentage points. A corrected interval wholly above zero supports the added learned component for the named algorithm within this benchmark. An interval crossing zero is inconclusive, not proof of equality. Intervals conservatively account for 248 comparisons, including prior exploratory work. These are eight paired training seeds per algorithm, not hundreds of thousands of independent vehicles.
2. **Full delivery.** Mean delivery among all five vehicles, including the two scouts. Four bars compare separately adapted routing actors under the same final schedules. The bar heights alone do not establish significance; use figures 1 and 5.
3. **Follower delivery.** Mean delivery among the three followers. This shows outcomes for vehicles that have less local hazard coverage and depend on scout messages. It is a descriptive secondary endpoint, not another corrected primary success criterion.
4. **Defined receiver utility.** Mean of `40*delivered - 40*destroyed - 40*censored - elapsed time`, per vehicle. It combines outcomes under stated weights. Higher utility does not imply every vehicle is faster or nominal routing cost always decreases. Common-survivor time/cost tables provide conditional descriptive comparisons.
5. **Every case-specific effect.** Each number is COMBINED delivery minus ALL, SAFE or VALUE in percentage points. An asterisk means the corrected lower interval bound exceeds zero; a dagger means the corrected upper bound is below zero. No symbol means the result does not establish either direction. All cases, including late warnings and no hazards, remain visible. A secondary case win cannot replace an inconclusive pooled primary endpoint.
6. **Variation across training seeds.** Each bar is a paired-seed pooled COMBINED minus SAFE difference. This reveals whether a mean gain depends on a few training outcomes. These bars are descriptive; they are not eight separate hypothesis tests or confidence intervals.
7. **Actual adaptation learning curves.** Training delivery averaged over eight seeds and smoothed over 30 episodes. Episodes 300–599 follow the 300-episode common ALL warmup. Curves show actual observed training outcomes, not fabricated progress or a mathematical convergence guarantee. Final test data never contribute to these curves.
8. **Fixed-actor gate swap.** Hold the COMBINED-trained actor fixed and compare SAFE versus COMBINED filtering on four additional layouts and fresh tapes. This is conditional on that actor's training and may favor its familiar gate; it does not compare both separately optimized systems or add independent training seeds. It diagnoses immediate message-filtering effects and is descriptive only.

Final test graphs alternate three and four forks; four-fork size is absent from training. Four corruption cases use training parameter values on fresh layouts/tapes, two introduce unseen corruption parameters, and two are controls. The eight cases receive equal weight in the primary endpoint. The setting remains a structured mechanism benchmark, not validation on operational logistics networks.

Receiver admission is distinct from transmission: rejecting more messages does not by itself reduce sent bandwidth. Claimed sender identities are unauthenticated. Confidence is reported evidence, not proof of honesty; model ensemble disagreement is a heuristic, not calibrated uncertainty coverage.
