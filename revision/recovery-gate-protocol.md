# Prospective dynamic-recovery gate study

Specified before new training, validation and final testing, 19 October 2026. This is a new constructed benchmark following the negative matched-training result and decision audit. It cannot retrospectively rescue the previous general-superiority claim. All earlier outputs remain available.

## Question and physical mechanism

Can a gate with bounded authority to both accept and reject messages improve fleet mission utility over specified conservative baselines while delivery is noninferior within one percentage point? The old audit found no eligible genuine clearances in its sampled trajectories. This study introduces actual temporary closures and physical sensor observations of reopening. No honest clearance is injected manually and no route is scripted.

Each stage begins with one of two cheap branches blocked. Independently, it is temporary with the case probability. Expiry is the earliest nominal arrival at the stage approach plus uniform 1.2–3.5 time units for temporary closures, or 35–50 for persistent ones. Two scouts have radius-two sensing, three followers have radius-one sensing with corridor blind spots. A scout that observes blocked then open broadcasts a clearance using the existing sensor and message implementation. Follower lead, sensor access, packet processing and physical losses are identical across methods. The expensive bypass is scaled uniformly 1–1.5 during training and 1–2 at testing. Later-stage closures can expire before a delayed convoy arrives; this is part of the declared process, not removed after outcomes.

Forged receiver traffic uses uniformly selected public candidate corridors, a fair blocked/clear payload independent of active hazards, the honest confidence distribution and 90% claimed-identity impersonation. Loss affects honest transmissions; forged traffic is receiver injection. The hidden recovery schedule, real origin and active hazard state are unavailable to the gate. Only physically measured own observations can contribute truth feedback.

## Methods, labels and training

ALL accepts every otherwise valid packet. SAFE admits warnings and requires two claimed sources to replace a peer warning with a clearance. KEEP rejects every peer clearance against a peer warning. AGE uses confidence and the age of a prior warning or two-source corroboration. Shared expiry, duplicate, timestamp and own-observation precedence checks apply first to every method.

LEARN falls back to SAFE. On a publicly reachable edge, it may override SAFE when predicted absolute utility benefit minus half the bootstrap standard deviation exceeds the margin, the corresponding estimated adverse-delivery probability is below the risk limit, and the receiver has used fewer than two overrides in that episode. Both accepting a useful clearance and rejecting an unhelpful warning are permitted. These risk estimates are heuristic and are **not calibrated safety guarantees**.

Thirty public features include timestamps, claimed-source agreement from later physical observations, corroboration, prior beliefs, nominal route and detour changes, and later packets in the current inbox. Nominal route changes are reference-plan features, not actual neural actor action probes. Three bootstrap nonlinear hurdle networks estimate impact probability, conditional receiver utility and three-class delivery change. Complete-inbox accept/reject rollouts provide labels with the frozen routing actor. Both branches retain later inbox packets and future events; the factual branch must reproduce all five convoy outcomes. Per episode select at most four clear-against-warning, two warning and two other eligible packets uniformly within these public strata, with no outcome/origin selection or unused-slot borrowing.

For each DQN/MAPPO algorithm and eight paired seeds, train 300 SAFE warmup episodes, then deepcopy the actor, optimizer, replay and RNG state into five arms. Each arm adapts for 300 episodes on identical environment tapes. Thus every final actor sees 600 routing episodes. LEARN additionally fits 64 initial factual label episodes and, after 150 adaptation episodes, 64 new episodes with its current frozen actor. Fit uses the first 56 episodes of each collection; the last eight, balanced over conditions, are held out. The refreshed model combines old and fresh data; its ablation cannot isolate staleness from extra data. Auxiliary data and fitting cost are extra and disclosed.

## Selection and locked testing

Before final test, eight fresh validation layouts and two episodes in each of eight training conditions select inference settings separately by algorithm, pooled across all eight seeds. LEARN tries risk limits .05/.15/.30 and margins 0/1. AGE tries prior-warning ages 1/2/4/8 and confidences .4/.6/.8. Select greatest validation utility among options with delivery at least SAFE minus one percentage point; if none qualify select greatest delivery, then utility. Exact ties use candidate order. Actors were trained with fixed defaults, so this is inference setting selection, not retraining per candidate. Freeze settings, checkpoint hashes and estimator hashes before test.

Final testing uses 16 fresh layouts, eight conditions, ten environment episodes, eight seeds, two algorithms and five methods: **102,400 method episodes and 512,000 convoy records**. Four-fork size is held out from training. The conditions are persistent, mixed and rapid recovery; held-out loss, delay and corruption; late communication and no-hazard controls. All are reported. Layout/tape namespaces and machine-readable settings are in `run_recovery_gate_study.py::DESIGN` and the generated `design.json`.

The primary endpoint is fleet mean utility `40*delivered - 40*destroyed - 40*censored - elapsed`, including every convoy; fleet delivery is a co-required noninferiority endpoint. For LEARN versus each SAFE/KEEP/AGE baseline and each algorithm, success requires corrected utility lower bound > 0 **and** corrected delivery lower bound > −1 percentage point. The one-point tolerance is a declared simulation engineering tolerance, not operational endorsement. There are 12 primary contrasts. Secondary comparisons are both metrics versus ALL/SAFE/KEEP/AGE for all eight conditions and both algorithms, 128 contrasts. All 140 new intervals use 200,000 crossed seed/layout/within-layout episode bootstrap draws and two-sided Bonferroni correction 388, counting 248 earlier comparisons conservatively. Earlier published intervals are not recomputed; no updated joint historical coverage is asserted.

A descriptive fixed-LEARN-actor swap among deployed LEARN, rejection-only LEARN and the initial estimator runs on four further layouts with five episodes per condition: 7,680 method episodes. This is not an independent trained-policy comparison. No post-test tuning, case omission, guarantee of universal superiority, convergence or operational transfer is permitted. Negative results remain evidence.
