# Proposed manuscript claim and evidence

This is draft replacement text supported by the executed studies, not a revised manuscript or a completed point-by-point reviewer response.

## Defensible replacement claim

> In randomized physical scout–follower routing networks, safety-constrained actor-specific message-value filtering improves the delivery of frozen DQN and MAPPO actors over their communication-ALL and validation-tuned FRC counterparts under a specified, previously unseen balanced cover-traffic corruption shift. With a forged-report rate of six per simulation time unit, message delay one, loss 0.1 and scout lead four, full-convoy delivery increases from 77.17% to 85.20% for DQN and from 71.63% to 78.77% for MAPPO. The corresponding paired differences against ALL are 8.03 percentage points (simultaneous interval 0.84–16.10) and 7.13 points (0.74–16.40), using 200,000 bootstrap draws and a conservative 198-comparison correction across the reported study stages. These results apply to the declared structured layouts, sensing roles, corruption process and pretrained actors; they do not establish universal gate superiority.

All vehicles count in these percentages: two scouts and three followers. Follower-only delivery is reported separately. ALL and tuned FRC have the same heavy-cover delivery outcomes in this test, so the two comparisons are not independent replication of the effect.

Loss 0.1 refers to honest broadcasts; forged packets represent receiver-side injection without stochastic transport loss. State this threat-model assumption alongside the quantitative claim.

## What the learned component establishes

The initial unconstrained value model sometimes rejected useful warnings. The final gate preserves fresh confident public-reachable hazard reports and requires corroboration before warning erasure, while retaining learned actor-specific utility admission for other reports. The models use complete-episode chronological-prefix intervention labels from training schedules; no test labels enter model fitting. Source hashes, fitted weights, causal examples, internal held-out errors and separate numeric selection records are delivered.

The simple SAFE-ALL comparator is essential. Under heavy immediate cover traffic, DQN VALUE achieves 85.20% versus SAFE-ALL 84.07%, and MAPPO VALUE 78.77% versus SAFE-ALL 77.80%. Their differences are **not** supported after correction: intervals −0.70–4.90 and −1.20–4.80 percentage points. Therefore the neural result supports the guarded filtering mechanism over ALL/FRC, rather than proving that learned utility outperforms every conservative alternative.

There is a separate learned-utility benefit with the model-based routing actor. In the primary multiple-impersonation case, VALUE delivers 95.1%, ALL/FRC 75.0% and SAFE-ALL 90.0%; the corrected VALUE versus SAFE-ALL comparison is supported. In the balanced heavy-cover stress, the planner achieves VALUE 87.5% versus SAFE-ALL 84.7%, with difference 2.8 points and corrected interval 0.20–5.80. Do not relabel these planner results as MAPPO or AMMARL neural performance.

## Reviewer concerns addressed by this work

| Concern | Implemented evidence | Remaining limit |
|---|---|---|
| Messages previously failed to influence routing | Physical leading scouts, blind fork corridors, feasible detours and fresh held-out cases | Small structured mechanisms; original-topology transfer remains separate |
| Gate value was a heuristic planner proxy | Actor-specific utility labels from complete-episode inclusion/omission replays | Conditional prefix labels and off-policy distribution shift, not optimal causal allocation |
| Apparent benefit could come from a weak baseline | Same actor weights and interpreter; FRC tuned on validation; SAFE-ALL included | History-aware or recurrent ALL after retraining on cover traffic is not tested |
| Neural learning was previously reused without new training | Six fresh DQN/MAPPO actors, 600 episodes each, real learning curves | Three independent training seeds per algorithm; no convergence proof |
| Attacker behavior could expose its own targets | Balanced forged targets/payloads independent of live truth; new cover-traffic stress | A declared corruption shift, not every adaptive adversary |
| Claimed improvements lacked matched uncertainty estimates | Paired tapes/targets/departures; graph/episode clustering and crossed training seeds; final 198-test correction | Bootstrap support limited by ten layouts and three training seeds |
| Filtering could fail or induce extra detours | All initial adverse results, guard ablations, late/redundant/no-hazard controls and common-survivor costs retained | No claim of superiority on every KPI or guaranteed noninferiority |

## Claims to remove or qualify

Remove universal superiority, “necessary and sufficient,” guaranteed convergence, perfect authenticity, optimal message valuation and all-KPI dominance. Do not replace the original manuscript's fixed headline table with a favorable single stress condition while retaining its original scenario description. Name the actor, benchmark, corruption shift, baseline, denominator, training status and interval for every new quantitative claim.

The full fresh evidence contains 213,220 evaluation episodes and 1,066,100 convoy records across three stages, but **only three independent training seeds per neural algorithm**. Later stages reuse training-only models and introduce fresh test layouts/tapes; they are not additional independent actor training runs. Earlier failed/negative results remain part of the record.
